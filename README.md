# Causality-Assessment

## Objective
To evaluate the relationship between a suspected drug and an adverse drug reaction using standard causality assessment methods.

## Case Study

Patient: Raj

Suspected Drug: Amoxicillin

Indication: Throat infection

### Case Timeline

- 01-Jun-2021: Amoxicillin 500 mg three times daily was started for throat infection.
- 02-Jun-2021: The patient developed itchy rashes over his body.
- 03-Jun-2021: The dose was reduced to 250 mg three times daily.
- 04-Jun-2021: The rash disappeared.
- 05-Jun-2021: Due to increased intensity of infection, the dose was increased to 500 mg three times daily.
- 06-Jun-2021: The patient again developed generalized itchy rashes.

## Naranjo Adverse Drug Reaction Probability Assessment

| Question | Answer | Score |
|---|---|---:|
| Previous conclusive reports on this reaction? | Yes | +1 |
| Did the event appear after the suspected drug was administered? | Yes | +2 |
| Did the reaction improve after the drug was discontinued or dose was reduced? | Yes | +1 |
| Did the reaction reappear after re-administration? | Yes | +2 |
| Are there alternative causes? | No | +2 |
| Did the reaction reappear with placebo? | Do not know | 0 |
| Was the drug detected at toxic concentrations? | Do not know | 0 |
| Was the reaction more severe with increased dose or less severe with decreased dose? | Yes | +1 |
| Similar reaction in previous exposure? | Do not know | 0 |
| Was the adverse event confirmed by objective evidence? | Yes | +1 |

### Total Score

Naranjo Score = 10

## Naranjo Classification

A score of 10 falls under the:

**Definite category (≥9)**

## WHO-UMC Causality Assessment

**Category: Certain / Definite**

### Reason

There is a clear temporal relationship between amoxicillin administration and the development of the rash. The reaction improved after dose reduction and reappeared after the dose was increased again. This positive rechallenge and dose-response relationship support a strong causal association between amoxicillin and the adverse reaction.

## Conclusion

The adverse reaction is classified as **Definite** based on the Naranjo probability scale, with a total score of 10. The clinical course also supports a **Certain/Definite** causal relationship according to the WHO-UMC causality assessment.
